# Recent Progress in Upper Bounds on Matrix Multiplication and Theoretical Barriers to Omega = 2

## Key findings

- Since 2020, the upper bound on the matrix multiplication exponent has dropped through four consecutive breakthroughs: from 2.37287 down to 2.37286, then to 2.371866, 2.371552, and most recently to 2.371339. [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)[[2]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970)[[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173)[[4]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05846)
- Initial progress in 2020 stemmed from a probabilistic refinement to the laser method that salvages tensor components sharing identical marginal distributions, improving the retained component ratio from a linear penalty to a square-root penalty. [[4]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05846)[[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173)
- The larger leaps since 2023 arose from identifying and mitigating combination loss across recursive tensor powers via asymmetric hashing, which permits tensor components to share variable blocks in one or two dimensions while preserving independence. [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)[[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173)[[2]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970)[[5]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06535)
- Extensive barrier theorems demonstrate that these laser-method techniques applied to fixed intermediate tensors, such as the Coppersmith-Winograd tensor, cannot achieve omega = 2, and in fact face explicit lower bounds including a 2.3078 limit on the fifth tensor power. [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5414)[[7]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06952)[[8]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08671)[[9]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03019)[[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)

## Progression of Upper Bounds on the Exponent Since 2020

In 2020, Alman and Vassilevska Williams lowered the long-standing upper bound on the matrix multiplication exponent from 2.37287 to 2.37286 (specifically 2.3728596) by improving the zeroing-out step in the laser method. [[4]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05846)[[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173)[[10]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.7714) In 2023, Duan, Wu, and Zhou established an upper bound of 2.371866 by analyzing the eighth power of the Coppersmith-Winograd tensor and breaking the previously believed intermediate barrier of 2.3725. [[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173)[[2]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970)[[5]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06535)[[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349) Williams, Xu, Xu, and Zhou further reduced the upper bound to 2.371552 while simultaneously proving improved bounds for rectangular matrix multiplication exponents, including establishing the dual exponent alpha strictly greater than 0.321334. [[2]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970)[[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349) In 2024, Alman, Duan, Vassilevska Williams, Xu, Xu, and Zhou achieved the current record upper bound of 2.371339 by incorporating deeper asymmetry into the tensor zeroing-out machinery. [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)[[11]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20538)

## Algorithmic Refinements of the Laser Method

The 2020 refinement redesigned the resolution of multi-distribution collisions during zeroing-out; when several distributions share the same marginals, random subset selection allows retaining a Theta(B / sqrt(m)) fraction of desired components rather than the greedy Theta(B / m) fraction. [[4]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05846) The breakthrough by Duan, Wu, and Zhou uncovered 'combination loss', where previous recursive analyses discarded whole variable blocks across recursive levels even when the underlying constituent sub-blocks were mutually disjoint and independent. [[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173)[[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)[[5]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06535) To salvage these combinations, researchers introduced asymmetric hashing, treating one or more tensor coordinate directions differently to allow components to share variable blocks along selected axes while maintaining overall independence. [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)[[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173)[[2]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970) The 2024 advancement maximized asymmetry by requiring uniqueness only for variable blocks in the first dimension, allowing shared blocks in both remaining dimensions at higher recursion levels and enforcing uniqueness only at the base level. [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)

## Barrier Theorems and Obstacles to Reaching Omega = 2

Ambainis, Filmus, and Le Gall proved that the laser method with merging applied to the Coppersmith-Winograd tensor CW5 cannot prove an exponent below 2.3078, establishing a definitive ceiling on refinements within that structural class. [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5414)[[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16349)[[2]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970)[[3]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10173) Alman and Vassilevska Williams generalized these limits to the 'Solar' and 'Galactic' frameworks, demonstrating that any approach relying on zeroing-out or monomial degenerations of fixed cyclic group tensors or Coppersmith-Winograd variants cannot yield omega = 2 for any fixed base parameter. [[8]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08671)[[12]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.07246) Christandl, Vrana, and Zuiddam established an irreversibility barrier showing that intermediate tensors exhibit an asymptotic transformation loss; because the irreversibility of any fixed Coppersmith-Winograd tensor strictly exceeds one, reductions through them cannot prove omega = 2. [[7]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06952)[[9]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03019) Corresponding degeneration barriers also limit the dual exponent alpha for rectangular matrix multiplication, proving that no degenerations from CWq can achieve an alpha exceeding 0.625. [[9]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03019)[[2]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970)

## Limitations

- Known barrier results apply specifically to fixed intermediate tensors, monomial degenerations, or laser-method frameworks, leaving open whether variable tensor families or arbitrary degenerations could theoretically achieve omega = 2.
- Non-convex optimization programs for computing parameters over higher tensor powers scale rapidly in complexity, restricting practical evaluation beyond small tensor powers.
- The asymptotic improvements do not yield practical algorithms due to astronomically large leading constants driven by arithmetic progression-free sets and recursive tensor power embeddings.
