# GLP-1 and Dual Incretin Receptor Agonists Beyond Weight Loss: Evidence from Post-2020 Randomised Controlled Trials

## Key findings

- In major cardiovascular randomised controlled trials, semaglutide demonstrated a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in overweight or obesity without diabetes (SELECT) and superiority for MACE with oral semaglutide in type 2 diabetes (SOUL), while both semaglutide (STEP-HFpEF) and tirzepatide (SUMMIT) significantly reduced worsening heart failure events and improved symptoms in obesity-related heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555826)[[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556714)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13009101)[[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13314376)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39397557)
- For renal protection, semaglutide reduced primary composite kidney events, progressive loss of kidney function, and cardiorenal death in dedicated trials (FLOW) and across pooled analyses of over 30,000 trial participants (SELECT, FLOW, SOUL), while tirzepatide significantly slowed eGFR decline and reduced albuminuria in post-hoc randomised analyses and active-comparator trials. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42567173)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36152639)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12640882)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39746157)[[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36651820)
- In addiction medicine, a proof-of-concept randomised trial showed low-dose semaglutide reduced alcohol self-administration in the laboratory, drinks per drinking day, and craving in alcohol use disorder, but meta-analyses of existing human trials find no statistically significant pooled effect across alcohol or tobacco cessation endpoints, reflecting small sample sizes and exploratory study designs. [[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11822619)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13409016)[[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085)[[14]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC10721313)[[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12808432)
- Critical questions remain unresolved, including the lack of dedicated primary kidney outcome trials for dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists, the comparative cardiovascular superiority of dual agonism versus selective GLP-1 receptor agonism beyond non-inferiority, the long-term durability of benefits following treatment withdrawal, and efficacy across diverse substance use disorders. [[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13341370)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13409016)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203)[[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36152639)

## Cardiovascular Protection and Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction

In adults with overweight or obesity and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease without diabetes, the SELECT randomised trial established that semaglutide 2.4 mg once weekly reduced the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% compared with placebo. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13314376)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39397557) In patients with type 2 diabetes and high cardiovascular risk, the phase 3 SOUL randomised controlled trial demonstrated that oral semaglutide achieved a statistically significant 14% reduction in MACE compared with placebo (hazard ratio 0.86), an effect primarily driven by a 26% reduction in nonfatal myocardial infarction. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13009101) In the double-blind randomised SUMMIT trial evaluating patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) and obesity, tirzepatide significantly reduced the risk of the primary composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events by 38% compared with placebo (hazard ratio 0.62), primarily driven by a 46% relative reduction in worsening heart failure events. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555826)[[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556714)[[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13341370) Expanded analyses of the SUMMIT trial revealed comprehensive clinical improvements with tirzepatide in HFpEF, including significant enhancements in Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire scores (between-group difference 6.9 points), 6-minute walk distance (+18.3 metres), and New York Heart Association functional class. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556714)[[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555826) The active-comparator SURPASS-CVOT randomised trial in patients with type 2 diabetes and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease demonstrated that tirzepatide met the prespecified non-inferiority criterion for 3-point MACE compared with dulaglutide (hazard ratio 0.92), with exploratory analyses showing a 16% reduction in all-cause mortality. [[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13341370)[[18]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13049956)

## Renal Outcomes and Preservation of Kidney Function

The dedicated phase 3b FLOW randomised controlled trial demonstrated that once-weekly semaglutide 1.0 mg significantly reduced the composite endpoint of kidney failure, persistent 50% or greater eGFR reduction, and cardiorenal death in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease. [[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36651820)[[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13314376)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12640882)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39397557) A prespecified participant-level pooled analysis of 30,787 individuals from the FLOW, SELECT, and SOUL randomised trials demonstrated that semaglutide reduced the first occurrence of the primary kidney composite by 16% (hazard ratio 0.84) and a narrower kidney-specific composite excluding cardiovascular death by 20% (hazard ratio 0.80) across diverse cardiometabolic risk groups. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42567173)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12640882) In a post-hoc analysis of the phase 3 SURPASS-4 randomised trial, tirzepatide significantly slowed the annual rate of eGFR decline compared with insulin glargine (-1.4 versus -3.6 mL/min/1.73 m2 per year) and lowered the risk of the composite kidney outcome by 42% (hazard ratio 0.58). [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36152639)[[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC10039543) A pooled post-hoc analysis of the SURPASS 1 to 5 trials showed that tirzepatide reduced the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) by 19.3% to 26.3% across doses versus pooled comparators, with mediation analyses suggesting that approximately half of the albuminuria reduction was weight-loss dependent. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39746157)

## Substance Use Disorders and Addiction Endpoints

In a randomized clinical trial among adults with alcohol use disorder, low-dose semaglutide significantly reduced alcohol consumption and peak breath alcohol concentration during a post-treatment laboratory self-administration task, as well as reducing drinks per drinking day and weekly craving, without altering the proportion of abstinent days. [[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11822619)[[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085) A secondary analysis of a randomised, placebo-controlled trial evaluating dulaglutide during smoking cessation found that participants receiving dulaglutide drank 29% less alcohol at week 12 compared with placebo (relative effect 0.71), an effect independent of smoking abstinence. [[14]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC10721313)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203) A systematic review and meta-analysis of five randomised controlled trials found no statistically significant pooled effect of GLP-1 receptor agonists on days without alcohol consumption, cigarettes smoked per day, or nicotine dependence severity scores, rating the overall certainty of evidence as low to moderate due to substantial statistical heterogeneity and small sample sizes. [[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13409016)[[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12808432) Systematic evaluation of clinical trial registries indicates that clinical research examining GLP-1 receptor agonists in addiction remains predominantly early-phase, with large phase 3 trials in alcohol use disorder (such as CRAVE) and trials evaluating cannabis and stimulant use disorders currently underway. [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085)[[20]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07218354)[[21]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07523633)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203)[[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12808432)

## Unresolved Questions and Future Directions

Tirzepatide has not yet been evaluated in dedicated, event-driven primary renal outcome randomised trials, leaving its independent renoprotective efficacy reliant on post-hoc analyses and secondary trial endpoints. [[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13341370)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36152639)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39746157) While SURPASS-CVOT confirmed cardiovascular non-inferiority between tirzepatide and dulaglutide, whether dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonism provides superior cardiovascular protection over selective GLP-1 receptor agonists remains unproven. [[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13341370)[[18]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13049956) The efficacy, optimal dosing, and safety of GLP-1 receptor agonists in non-overweight populations with substance use disorders remain unresolved, particularly regarding the clinical risks of unneeded weight loss. [[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11822619)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13409016) Whether the observed cardiorenal and behavioral improvements persist after cessation of GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy or require indefinite long-term administration has not been established in prospective trials. [[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13409016)

## Limitations

- Most randomized controlled trial data for GLP-1 receptor agonists in substance use disorders are derived from small pilot studies with short treatment windows (6 to 26 weeks) and show substantial heterogeneity.
- Dedicated primary renal outcome trials are available for semaglutide (FLOW) but absent for tirzepatide, where kidney evidence is limited to post-hoc analyses and secondary endpoints.
- Head-to-head cardiovascular outcome evidence comparing tirzepatide directly against GLP-1 receptor agonists is currently restricted to an active-comparator non-inferiority design (SURPASS-CVOT).
- Evidence for GLP-1 receptor agonists in cannabis, cocaine, and opioid use disorders is largely limited to preclinical models or ongoing registered trials without published randomized results.
- The persistence of cardiorenal and neuropsychiatric benefits following treatment discontinuation remains uncharacterized in randomized withdrawal trials.
