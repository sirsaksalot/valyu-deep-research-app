# GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Beyond Weight Loss: Evidence from Post-2020 Randomised Controlled Trials and Unresolved Questions

## Key findings

- In large cardiovascular outcome trials and heart failure studies, semaglutide and tirzepatide significantly reduce major adverse cardiovascular events and heart failure exacerbations, while improving functional status and quality of life in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39181597)[[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12445413)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42569733)[[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41862384)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38985093)
- The landmark FLOW randomised controlled trial demonstrated that once-weekly semaglutide reduces major kidney disease events by 24% and slows the rate of eGFR decline in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, supported by preliminary trial evidence showing substantial albuminuria reductions in non-diabetic chronic kidney disease. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38985093)[[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39127031)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39455729)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12640882)
- Randomised clinical trial evidence for addiction remains in early stages: a hybrid phase 2 trial showed that low-dose semaglutide significantly reduced laboratory alcohol self-administration, craving, and drinks per drinking day in adults with alcohol use disorder, though broader addiction evidence still largely relies on preclinical and observational data. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11822619)[[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12808432)[[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203)
- Major unresolved questions include the efficacy of GLP-1 receptor agonists in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, additive cardiorenal benefits alongside SGLT2 inhibitors or mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, long-term hard clinical endpoints in non-diabetic renal disease, and safety in normal-weight individuals treated for substance use disorders. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38985093)[[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12166642)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11822619)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12640882)[[14]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07738276)

## Cardiovascular and Heart Failure Outcomes

In a prespecified analysis of the double-blind randomised SELECT trial, semaglutide 2.4 mg significantly lowered major adverse cardiovascular events (hazard ratio 0.72) and composite heart failure events (hazard ratio 0.79) in patients with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity, with consistent benefit observed across heart failure subtypes. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39181597)[[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12445413) Randomised controlled trials evaluating heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, such as STEP-HFpEF for semaglutide and the SUMMIT trial for tirzepatide, demonstrated significant improvements in 6-minute walk distance, Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire scores, and reductions in worsening heart failure events or cardiovascular death. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12445413)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42569733)[[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41862384)[[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12166642) Real-world target trial emulations and meta-analyses corroborate these findings, indicating substantial reductions in heart failure hospitalisations and mortality, though observational head-to-head analyses indicate no statistically significant difference in hospitalisation or all-cause mortality between tirzepatide and semaglutide. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41862384)[[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40886075)[[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40368924)

## Renoprotective Outcomes in Chronic Kidney Disease

The FLOW randomised controlled trial in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease on background renin-angiotensin system blockade demonstrated that semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly decreased the primary composite kidney endpoint by 24% (hazard ratio 0.76) and slowed annual eGFR loss by 1.16 mL/min/1.73 m2, prompting early trial cessation for efficacy. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38985093)[[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39127031)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12640882) In a 24-week double-blind randomised trial of adults with overweight or obesity and chronic kidney disease without diabetes, semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly reduced the urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio by 52.1% compared with placebo. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39455729) While dedicated randomised trials assessing hard kidney endpoints for tirzepatide are limited, post-hoc analyses of randomised trials such as SURPASS-4 and SURMOUNT-1/2, alongside real-world cohort studies, report substantial reductions in albuminuria and lower risks of end-stage renal disease. [[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13314376)[[18]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03730662)

## Addiction and Substance Use Disorders

A double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial in non-treatment-seeking adults with alcohol use disorder demonstrated that semaglutide significantly reduced voluntary alcohol intake during a laboratory self-administration task, weekly craving scores, and drinks per drinking day, accompanied by exploratory decreases in daily cigarette consumption. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11822619)[[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085) Systematic reviews of substance use trials note that earlier randomised clinical trials with older GLP-1 receptor agonists showed mixed findings, including a trial where exenatide reduced heavy drinking only within a high-BMI subgroup and dulaglutide trials failed to demonstrate increased smoking cessation rates. [[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12808432)[[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203) Target trial emulations using electronic health records indicate that initiation of tirzepatide or semaglutide is associated with a significantly reduced incidence of new-onset alcohol use disorder diagnoses compared to dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors. [[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12673470)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203)

## Unresolved Questions and Future Directions

The efficacy and safety of GLP-1 receptor agonists across the full spectrum of heart failure remain uncertain, as historical trials of older agents raised safety concerns in reduced ejection fraction, leading to ongoing randomised trials such as DUAL-HFrEF evaluating tirzepatide in this cohort. [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12166642)[[14]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07738276)[[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12066815) Because only a minority of participants in pivotal kidney trials were receiving sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors and none received non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, the incremental benefit of semaglutide on top of modern guideline-directed multi-drug therapy is unresolved. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38985093)[[21]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38943325) The therapeutic utility of semaglutide and dual incretin agonists for substance use disorders remains investigational, pending results from multiple active and planned phase 2 and phase 3 trials evaluating diverse populations, dosing schedules, and long-term abstinence. [[22]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07218354)[[23]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06015893)[[24]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05892432)[[25]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07772960)[[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12906085) Safety and tolerability in non-obese, normal-weight populations treated for neuropsychiatric or addictive indications remain critical unknowns due to the potential risks of unwanted weight loss and gastrointestinal adverse events. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11822619)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39049203)[[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12808432)

## Limitations

- Most high-grade randomised evidence for renal and cardiovascular outcomes derives from semaglutide; dedicated event-driven cardiovascular and renal outcome trials for tirzepatide have only recently completed or remain ongoing.
- Data evaluating semaglutide in non-diabetic chronic kidney disease are limited to surrogate biomarker endpoints (albuminuria) over 24 weeks rather than hard clinical outcomes like dialysis or end-stage kidney disease.
- Addiction evidence in humans is predominantly constrained to small pilot or phase 2 trials, secondary analyses, and observational target trial emulations; definitive phase 3 trials in substance use disorders are still recruiting or pending publication.
- Randomised clinical trials directly comparing tirzepatide against semaglutide for clinical cardiovascular and renal endpoints are currently lacking.
- Neurodegenerative indications, such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases, rely primarily on preclinical data, older incretin agonists, and ongoing phase 3 trials without final published outcomes for semaglutide.
