# Randomised Controlled Trials Since 2020 on Semaglutide and Tirzepatide Beyond Weight Loss

## Key findings

- In patients with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease without diabetes, the phase 3 SELECT trial demonstrated that once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg significantly reduces major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% compared to placebo. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37952131)[[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38103177)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38066700)[[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39181597)
- For heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, semaglutide in the STEP-HFpEF program and tirzepatide in the phase 3 SUMMIT trial substantially improved symptom scores, functional exercise capacity, and reduced worsening heart failure events. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555826)[[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556714)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12333672)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12445413)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11835708)
- In patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, the landmark phase 3 FLOW trial established that semaglutide reduces major kidney disease events by 24%, slows eGFR decline, and reduces cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. [[10]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347?url_ver=Z39.88-2003&amp=&rfr_id=ori:rid:crossref.org&amp=&rfr_dat=cr_pub++0pubmed)[[11]](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/semaglutide-significantly-reduces-risk-of-renal-events-mace-mortality-in-people-with-ckd-t2d-in-final-flow-findings)[[12]](https://irvine.health/blogs/semaglutide-kidney/)[[13]](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/48/11/1878/163056/Effects-of-Semaglutide-With-or-Without-Concomitant)[[14]](https://www.biospace.com/novo-nordisk-s-semaglutide-cuts-death-risk-in-diabetics-with-ckd-in-phase-iii-trial)
- Evidence for kidney protection with tirzepatide stems primarily from post-hoc and pooled analyses of the SURPASS trials, which showed significant reductions in albuminuria and attenuation of eGFR decline compared to insulin glargine. [[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36152639)[[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39746157)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11208186)[[18]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39959269)
- Direct evidence for semaglutide and tirzepatide in substance use disorders remains preliminary or exploratory, with major human clinical trials in alcohol and cannabis use disorders still ongoing, while completed randomised trials of earlier GLP-1 receptor agonists showed mixed primary behavioral endpoints but positive neuroimaging and subgroup effects. [[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12155186)[[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC9675448)[[21]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC10721313)[[22]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36874396)[[23]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07218354)

## Cardiovascular and Heart Failure Outcomes

In the double-blind, event-driven SELECT trial involving 17,604 participants with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity without diabetes, subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly reduced the incidence of 3-point major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% over a mean follow-up of 39.8 months. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37952131)[[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38103177)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38066700)[[24]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32916609) Prespecified analyses of the SELECT trial demonstrated that semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events and composite heart failure events across participants with and without baseline clinical heart failure, benefiting both preserved and reduced ejection fraction subgroups. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39181597)[[25]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC8039387) In patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, the STEP-HFpEF and STEP-HFpEF DM trials showed that semaglutide produced clinically meaningful improvements in the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire score, 6-minute walk distance, systemic inflammatory markers, and heart failure hospitalization risk. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12333672)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12445413)[[26]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42342009)[[27]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42569733) In the double-blind phase 3 SUMMIT trial of 731 patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and obesity, the dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist tirzepatide reduced the composite risk of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure events by 38% and improved health-related quality of life and 6-minute walk distance. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555826)[[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556714)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11835708)[[27]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42569733) Mechanistic secondary analyses of the SUMMIT trial indicated that tirzepatide significantly lowered systolic blood pressure, estimated circulating blood volume, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, and cardiac injury biomarkers including troponin T. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11835708)[[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556714)

## Nephroprotection and Renal Function Preservation

The multicenter phase 3 FLOW trial in 3,533 patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease was stopped early for efficacy, demonstrating that semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly lowered the primary composite risk of kidney failure, persistent 50% eGFR decline, or kidney/cardiovascular death by 24%. [[10]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347?url_ver=Z39.88-2003&amp=&rfr_id=ori:rid:crossref.org&amp=&rfr_dat=cr_pub++0pubmed)[[11]](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/semaglutide-significantly-reduces-risk-of-renal-events-mace-mortality-in-people-with-ckd-t2d-in-final-flow-findings)[[12]](https://irvine.health/blogs/semaglutide-kidney/)[[14]](https://www.biospace.com/novo-nordisk-s-semaglutide-cuts-death-risk-in-diabetics-with-ckd-in-phase-iii-trial)[[28]](https://www.theisn.org/in-action/research/clinical-trials-isn-act-archive/the-flow-study-effects-of-semaglutide-on-chronic-kidney-disease-in-patients-with-type-2-diabetes/) FLOW trial results showed that semaglutide significantly slowed the annual rate of eGFR loss by 1.16 mL/min/1.73 m2, reduced 3-point MACE by 18%, and decreased all-cause mortality by 20%, with benefits preserved regardless of baseline mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist or SGLT2 inhibitor use. [[10]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347?url_ver=Z39.88-2003&amp=&rfr_id=ori:rid:crossref.org&amp=&rfr_dat=cr_pub++0pubmed)[[13]](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/48/11/1878/163056/Effects-of-Semaglutide-With-or-Without-Concomitant)[[11]](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/semaglutide-significantly-reduces-risk-of-renal-events-mace-mortality-in-people-with-ckd-t2d-in-final-flow-findings)[[12]](https://irvine.health/blogs/semaglutide-kidney/) In the SURPASS-4 open-label phase 3 trial, post-hoc analysis demonstrated that tirzepatide slowed the annual decline of eGFR by 2.2 mL/min/1.73 m2 and reduced the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio by 31.9% compared to titrated insulin glargine in patients with type 2 diabetes and elevated cardiovascular risk. [[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36152639)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11208186) A pooled post-hoc analysis of the SURPASS 1-5 trials revealed consistent dose-dependent reductions in urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio with tirzepatide, with mediation analyses suggesting that approximately half of this albuminuria reduction was independent of direct weight loss. [[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39746157)[[18]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39959269)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11208186)

## Addiction and Substance Use Disorder Investigations

Preclinical studies confirm that GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce self-administration of drugs and alcohol, including an experimental study demonstrating that semaglutide significantly reduced voluntary alcohol consumption in alcohol-preferring vervet monkeys. [[29]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11742737)[[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12155186)[[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC9675448) In a 26-week randomised controlled trial evaluating exenatide for alcohol use disorder, exenatide did not meet its primary endpoint of reducing heavy drinking days across the overall population, but fMRI confirmed attenuated alcohol cue reactivity in mesolimbic circuits and exploratory analyses found reduced drinking specifically in individuals with obesity. [[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC9675448)[[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12155186)[[29]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11742737) In a randomised double-blind trial evaluating dulaglutide for smoking cessation, dulaglutide failed to improve smoking abstinence rates over placebo, but a predefined secondary analysis demonstrated a 29% reduction in alcohol intake at week 12 among baseline alcohol consumers. [[22]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36874396)[[21]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC10721313)[[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC12155186) Direct randomised clinical trial evidence for semaglutide in addiction remains uncompleted, though phase 2 and 3 randomised trials are actively recruiting or ongoing to evaluate semaglutide for alcohol use disorder and cannabis use disorder. [[30]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06015893)[[23]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07218354)[[31]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07523633)[[32]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07223983)

## Unresolved Questions and Future Directions

The extent to which cardiorenal benefits are driven by direct receptor-mediated tissue effects versus indirect benefits from systemic fat mass reduction, hemodynamic unloading, and metabolic improvement remains unsettled. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38103177)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38066700)[[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39746157)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11835708) Dedicated hard renal endpoint outcome trials for tirzepatide in chronic kidney disease, especially in patients without type 2 diabetes, have not yet delivered definitive results, leaving a reliance on secondary and post-hoc diabetes analyses. [[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11208186)[[18]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39959269)[[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36152639) Whether oral formulations confer the same magnitude of organ protection is unclear, as the oral semaglutide SOUL trial demonstrated cardiovascular risk reduction but failed to show a statistically significant reduction in major kidney disease outcomes. [[33]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC13009101)[[25]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC8039387) The generalizability of major cardiovascular trials remains constrained by subgroup underrepresentation, such as the low enrollment of female (28%) and Black (4%) participants in the SELECT trial. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38103177)

## Limitations

- Published, dedicated randomised controlled trials testing semaglutide or tirzepatide directly for addiction endpoints in human substance use disorders are currently lacking; available evidence relies on earlier agents (exenatide, dulaglutide), animal models, or ongoing trial protocols.
- Renal evidence for tirzepatide is currently limited to post-hoc, exploratory, and pooled analyses of glycemic trials rather than dedicated primary renal outcome trials.
- Long-term post-cessation durability of incretin therapy across cardiovascular, renal, and addictive behaviors remains uncharacterized in clinical trial follow-ups.
- Underlying demographic disparities exist in landmark trials, including low enrollment of racial minorities and women in major cardiovascular outcome studies.
